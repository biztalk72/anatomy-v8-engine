# V8 / Eight cylinders, one rhythm

An open 90° V8 teaching engine turns on a machined display bed: eight pistons
reciprocate on four shared crossplane throws while the four-stroke charge and
idealized valves reveal why ignition happens every quarter-turn.

The figure uses the Anatomy projection and facing-dependent four-tone shading.
Cutaway barrel meshes extend the kit's solid vocabulary. All surfaces are opaque;
the front half of each barrel and the front crankcase wall are physically removed.
The color inside each chamber denotes its stroke, not a measured gas property.

## Planned assemblies (80 purposeful primary parts)

- Display bed: 1 bevelled bed, 4 feet, 4 top screw seats, 2 mount rails, 1 nameplate.
- Crankcase: 1 oil pan, 2 pan flanges, 2 end bearing stands, 5 main bearing caps.
- Crankshaft: 5 main journal segments, 4 crankpins, 8 crank cheeks, 1 flywheel,
  1 front hub.
- Cylinder assemblies: 8 cutaway barrels, 8 pistons, 8 connecting rods,
  8 cutaway head collars.
- Valvetrain: 8 paired valve assemblies and 8 spark plugs.

Small parts add ring grooves, wrist-pin bosses, big-end collars, bearing bolts,
flange screws, head bolts, cooling ribs, spark-plug ceramic lands, flywheel bolts
and timing marks. Parts share actual mounting surfaces.

## Physical conventions

Firing order 1–8–4–3–6–5–7–2, following the Chevrolet ZZ4 reference in the task.
Odd cylinders on one bank, even cylinders on the other. The visible front is the
negative-x end. Adjacent rods are staggered on a shared journal. Pin phases are
45°, 135°, 315°, 225°, giving four throws separated by 90°. R = 18 and L = 58
are teaching-model units, not dimensions of a particular production engine.
The barrel roof is at 90 units; piston crown is 10 units beyond the wrist pin.
The 36-unit stroke and 4-unit clearance give a symbolic 10:1 volume ratio.

Valve lift follows a smooth idealized envelope during each full 180° gas-exchange
stroke. Real valve advance/overlap, cam drive, rocker arms, cooling jackets,
manifolds, lubrication and thermodynamics are outside this cutaway's scope.
